Daily Brief privacy policy

Effective October 6, 2026

Daily Brief has no accounts, no advertising and no analytics. Nothing you enter is sent to us, and the app has no servers of its own.

Who is responsible

Daily Brief is made by Steibel Apps (Joseph Steibel). Contact: steibelapps@gmail.com.

What is stored on your iPhone

These are kept on the device and are not sent to us or to anyone else:

  • Your settings, and the last place the app found you, so it can open with your forecast.
  • Places and addresses you save, including the two ends of a commute.
  • Your garden: plants, planting dates, and any skyline you trace, with the spot where you stood.
  • Your wildlife and night-sky sightings, each with the time, the weather, and for wildlife the exact place you logged it.
  • Vehicles you add, articles you save, which articles you have opened, your reading log, and puzzle and trivia scores.
  • A weight, an age and a skin type, used only for on-screen comparisons and sunburn times. Weight and age start at a placeholder until you change them.
  • The date you first opened the app, kept in the iPhone’s keychain so the 14 days of full access are counted once.

Like any app’s data, this is included in your own iCloud or computer backup if you have backups turned on. It leaves the app only when you choose to share something: a shared card is a picture, and can show your town, dates and weather, never coordinates.

Calendar and reminders

With your permission the app reads your calendar and reminders and lets you add, edit and delete them. Those changes sync through your own calendar or reminders account, such as iCloud, the same way as changes made in Apple’s apps. If you turn on garden reminders, the app adds a list called Daily Brief to your Reminders.

Titles, notes and guests are never sent anywhere. There is one exception for locations: to show the drive to an event, the event’s location is sent to Apple’s maps service, and so is an address as you type it into an event. You can turn travel times off from any event that shows one.

If you turn on the morning notification, the title of your first event appears in it. Calendar and reminder widgets show titles on the Home Screen. They are set to hide titles while the iPhone is locked unless you turn on Show on Lock Screen in Settings. The app’s own Face ID lock does not cover widgets or notifications.

Camera, motion, Face ID and notifications

  • Camera. Used only while you trace your skyline, as a viewfinder. No photo or video is taken, saved or sent. What is saved is a set of angles.
  • Motion. The phone’s tilt and compass are used to aim at the sky and to trace a skyline. Nothing is recorded but those angles.
  • Face ID. If you turn on the app lock, iOS does the check and tells the app only yes or no.
  • Notifications. All of them are created on the iPhone. There is no push server.

What is sent, and to whom

To show local information the app asks public services for it directly from your iPhone. Each request carries what that service needs to answer and, like any internet request, your IP address. We never receive these requests. Widgets and weather alerts make the same requests in the background, without the app open.

  • Location, rounded. For the forecast, alerts, storm outlooks, river levels and nearby birds, frogs and reptiles, the app tells the US National Weather Service and other NOAA services, the US Geological Survey and the Global Biodiversity Information Facility roughly where you are: a point rounded to about a kilometer (a little over half a mile), or a wider area that is not centered on you. The forecast map asks NOAA for the area shown on screen, which opens a few miles off your position and follows the map if you move it. The EPA is sent your ZIP code for the UV index.
  • Location, exact, to Apple only. Apple’s maps and place-name services receive your actual position to find your town and ZIP code, for travel times and for address suggestions. Apple’s weather service receives it where the Weather Service has no forecast or cannot be reached. Addresses and towns you type are sent to Apple to be found on the map.
  • Your vehicle. If you add a vehicle to watch for recalls, its make, model and year are sent to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.
  • Books. When you look a book up, what you type is sent to Open Library.
  • News and recalls. Headlines are downloaded from each publisher’s or agency’s public feed, including feeds you add. To show a picture the app may also fetch the story’s page and image from the publisher. Opening a story loads the publisher’s website in a browser inside the app, where that site’s own cookies and privacy policy apply.
  • Everything else. Air quality (AirNow), space weather (NOAA), satellite orbits (CelesTrak), rocket launches (The Space Devs) and facts about the country of the day (Wikipedia and Wikidata) are fetched without anything about you in the request. “Read more” links open the source’s page in a browser inside the app.

Those services have their own privacy policies.

Trivia

The trivia questions are written for Daily Brief and ship with it, so getting them sends nothing to anyone.

A round played in Messages travels inside the link in the message: the questions and their answers, the names players typed, what each player picked, and a random code that tells the players in that round apart. Anyone with the link can see that round. Your scoreboard name and recent rounds are also kept on your phone.

The trivia web page runs in your browser. This site is hosted by GitHub, which like any web host sees your IP address when a page loads. The round itself stays in the link and is not sent to the server, and your name and recent rounds are kept in your browser’s storage. We receive nothing.

Purchases

Subscriptions are bought through Apple and renew until you cancel. We never see your payment details.

What we do not do

  • No tracking across apps or websites.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifier.
  • No analytics or crash reporting services.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information.

If you write to us

We receive your email address and whatever you send, and use it only to reply. Ask and we will delete the conversation.

Children

Daily Brief is not directed at children under 13. We do not collect personal information from anyone: nothing entered in the app is sent to us.

Changes

If the app changes what it does with information, this page will change first and the date above will be updated.
