Daily Brief

Your day on one screen: weather, calendar, reminders, news, tonight's sky, and the wildlife that lives near you.

Made by one dad, on morning walks

I’m Joe Steibel, a solo app developer in central Pennsylvania.

I work a full-time job and I’m a full-time dad to a young family. Daily Brief started as a way to keep up with my family: the calendar, the reminders and the weather, all together in one morning glance.

Most mornings I check it while walking our two mini goldendoodles, and on those walks I got curious about everything around me: the birds I could hear, the plants I passed, the planets and stars that would be overhead that night, and the frogs and crickets singing after dark. So I set out to see if Daily Brief could keep me up to date not only with my family’s life, but with the life around me too. It could, and now I’m sharing it with you.

How it’s made. I design Daily Brief, and Claude, an AI from Anthropic, writes the code under my direction. I decide what goes in, test every build on my own phone, and keep the standards.

Sources and credit. I felt strongly that every fact in the app had to be backed by sources, two that agree wherever two exist, and you can open them from any fact. I also wanted to own as much of the app’s information as I could, written and checked for Daily Brief and kept on your phone, rather than leaning on someone else’s service. Where I couldn’t, as with the weather, the star catalog and the animal recordings, I made sure the people and organizations behind that work are credited. The recordings are real, made by recordists who share them openly, and each one names its recordist on its card.

What it doesn’t do. No ads, no account, no tracking, and no data collected. Your calendar and reminders stay on your phone, and your location is rounded to about a kilometer before the app uses it to look up the weather, the sky and the wildlife near you.

Get help

Write to steibelapps+dailybrief@gmail.com. It helps to say which iPhone you have, your iOS version, and what you expected to happen. You can also send feedback from Settings inside the app.

Common questions

The weather or wildlife is for the wrong place

Daily Brief uses your location to find local weather, tonight's sky and the animals near you. Open the iPhone's Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Location Services, then Daily Brief, and choose While Using the App.

A widget looks out of date

iOS decides how often widgets refresh. Opening the app refreshes them right away. If one stays blank, remove it from the Home Screen and add it again.

Calendar or reminders are missing

Open the iPhone's Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Calendars (or Reminders), and give Daily Brief full access.

Managing your subscription

You get full access for your first 14 days. No payment details, no automatic charge. A subscription, once bought, renews until you cancel. Subscriptions are bought and billed through Apple: open the iPhone's Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, to change or cancel one.

Playing trivia with someone on Android

A trivia round started in Messages is sent as a link. Anyone without the app, on any phone or computer, can tap the link and play the same questions in a browser.

Where the information comes from

Weather comes from Apple Weather and the US National Weather Service. Wildlife ranges are built from public US Geological Survey, US Fish and Wildlife Service and NatureServe data, and local bird and frog lists from the Global Biodiversity Information Facility. Sources are credited inside the app.
