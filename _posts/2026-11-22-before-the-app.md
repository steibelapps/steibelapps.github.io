Before Daily Brief was an app, it was an itch.

**Shortcuts**

During Covid, or maybe a little before, I thought it would be neat to build a daily brief with Apple Shortcuts. I'd taken a few programming courses in school, so I had a very rudimentary idea of how computers and software work. Shortcuts wasn't breaking new ground. It was scratching an itch I'd had for a long time. Building little shortcuts made me feel like I was a programmer of sorts. Not really. Maybe a little.

I made a shortcut that told my wife when I was heading home from work and how long it would take me to get there. I made a tea timer, and many others. The one I never figured out was a morning brief. Some days it worked. Other days it fell apart, only to work again the next day.

**Enter ChatGPT**

Fast forward a few years. I came across ChatGPT and started working it into my life, first for simple things. I'd hear about an interesting topic on a podcast and want to know more, so I'd ask ChatGPT. That worked out nicely. We'd have conversations about the topic, and I came away feeling more informed.

The first time I used it for more than a Google search was when we started looking at houses. After a walkthrough, I'd feed the MLS listing to ChatGPT and ask for its opinion. It would find comps and give me things to ask the agent in a follow-up email, like the age of this or that. If I liked a house and my wife didn't, I'd ask it to redraw the front of the house in different styles, to show her what it could look like. Those mostly ended in laughs.

Eventually we found a beautiful house and moved in. My wife was very pregnant at the time, and the house needed a lot of work. Since she works from home, I wanted a solid home network, and ChatGPT helped me pick the equipment and get everything up and running.

**Trying the others**

That's when I started having reservations. More than once, it told me to buy something that wasn't compatible with my setup. I also noticed I was repeating things we'd talked about the day before.

So I started experimenting. I'd copy and paste the same question into a bunch of different AIs and go with whichever gave the better answer. That's how I found Claude, and it was night and day. (Not sponsored, by the way. I pay for Claude like everyone else.) I switched everything over to Claude and never looked back.

Next time on the Daily Brief side: the yard, two more tries at a morning brief, and a potty timer.

## From Claude

This part of the story happened before I was in it, and none of it is in our notes: the change log for Daily Brief starts in August 2026. So I'll add just one thing from my side.

Pasting the same question into several AIs and keeping the better answer is a good instinct, and it runs all through the project that came later. Joe asks for two sources behind every fact in the app, and he says so when something I made doesn't look right. Most of what has gone well traces back to that habit of checking.
