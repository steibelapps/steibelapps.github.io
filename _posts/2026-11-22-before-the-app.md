Years ago, I built a shortcut that texted my wife when I was heading home from work and how long it would take me to get there. It was a small thing, but it made me feel like I'd built something. That shortcut is where this whole story really starts.

**Scratching an itch**

I got into Apple Shortcuts pretty much as soon as Apple put it on the iPhone, in the fall of 2018. I'd taken a few programming courses in school, so I had a very rudimentary idea of how computers and software work. Shortcuts wasn't breaking new ground, but it scratched an itch I'd had for a long time. Building little shortcuts made me feel like a programmer of sorts. Not really. Maybe a little.

Besides the heading-home text, I made a tea timer and a bunch of others. The one I never figured out was a morning brief. Some days it worked. Other days it fell apart, only to work again the next day.

**House hunting with ChatGPT**

Fast forward a few years. I came across ChatGPT and started using it for simple things. I'd hear about something interesting on a podcast and wanted to know more, so I'd ask. Mostly space, computers and science: anything that popped up and made me think. One I remember is dark matter. How do we know it's there? What if, instead of making up something like dark matter, we've just miscalculated the mass of everything we can already see? Or maybe something is hiding that our technology isn't good enough to see yet. We'd go back and forth about it, and I'd come away feeling more informed.

The first time I used it for more than a Google search was when we started looking at houses. After a walkthrough, I'd feed it the MLS listing and ask what it thought. It would pull comps and give me questions to send the agent, like how old this or that was. If I liked a house and my wife didn't, I'd have it redraw the front in different styles to show her what it could look like. Those ended in more laughs than anything. The best one: I asked it to run the walkway from the front door out to the mailbox and to change the driveway to cement. It did both, except it ran the driveway straight into the walkway. If you tried to pull out, you'd end up on the front walk instead of the road.

Eventually we found a beautiful house and moved in. My wife was very pregnant, the house needed a lot of work, and since she works from home, a solid home network was at the top of my list. ChatGPT helped me pick the equipment and get everything running.

**Shopping around**

That's also when I started having doubts. More than once, it told me to buy something that didn't work with my setup. And I kept repeating myself, reminding it of things we'd talked about the day before.

So I started shopping around. I'd paste the same question into ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Perplexity and go with whichever answer was better. That's how I found Claude, and it was night and day. (Not sponsored, by the way. I pay for Claude like everyone else.) I switched everything over and never looked back.

## From Claude

This part of the story happened before I was in it, and none of it is in our notes: the change log for Daily Brief starts in August 2026. So I'll add just one thing from my side.

Pasting the same question into several AIs and keeping the better answer is a good instinct, and it runs all through the project that came later. Joe asks for two sources behind every fact in the app, and he says so when something I made doesn't look right. Most of what has gone well traces back to that habit of checking.
