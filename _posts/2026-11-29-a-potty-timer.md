The baby arrived, and so did the renovations. Life got a lot busier.

**The house**

We bought the house in October, and the upstairs needed work right away: patching drywall, pulling and repainting baseboards, painting every wall. I'd never done most of it, so I asked Claude. It told us what paint to buy and walked us through every step in order, and every step went into our reminders.

That was only the start. It showed me how to pull up carpet and tack strips, get old pet urine out of a concrete slab, run Ethernet through the walls and wire the jacks, swap a toilet without a mess, hang doors, change locks, caulk windows and winterize the house. When we needed big work done, I sent it the competing bids and asked which was best and what to ask before signing. It took over our monthly budget too: an hour of sorting statements became five minutes. And when the kids got sick, I'd run their symptoms by it to help decide whether to call the doctor.

**The yard**

We didn't really walk the yard until the next spring, and it was rough: crabgrass, weeds, bugs, bare spots, landscaping rocks everywhere and a few invasive trees. Between the yard and the basement, which we're still finishing, I had 15 to 30 minutes at a time, so I asked Claude for a plan. It sorted the jobs by how long and how noisy they were and put each one on the right day: when to spread weed killer, fertilizer and insect killer, when to dethatch, overseed and cut down trees. Every reminder had the steps in it and the why in the notes, so I could check its reasoning instead of just taking its word.

**The walk**

Most mornings started with a walk: two mini goldendoodles and a baby stroller, the leashes in one hand and my phone in the other. That walk was when I planned my day. I wanted to be a more involved husband and a more aware dad, which meant knowing what all of us had going on, today and this week. I also checked the weather against my reminders: rain today meant indoor jobs, and rain in a day or two meant it was time to fertilize. It was a juggling act, pushing a stroller with two puppies pulling me toward every squirrel, rabbit or shadow.

Keeping track of all of it with a newborn at home and very little sleep was tough. I needed everything in one place instead of spread across three apps, and that's when I thought a daily brief would really help.

**Two more tries**

First I asked Claude to put one together for me each morning: weather, calendar, reminders and local events. It was ten times better than Shortcuts, but some mornings it skipped the calendar, and all those searches used up my limits fast. Then I spent a weekend with Claude building it in Shortcuts. We got further than I ever had alone, but it still crashed and burned.

**The potty timer**

Around then we started potty training our son. He had to sit for a minute each time, and he loved watching the countdown timer on our phones. Then he decided he wanted it in different colors. I couldn't find a free app that did that, so I asked Claude to make one: a countdown he could recolor by tapping buttons on my phone. It made potty training that much easier.

**On the swing**

Not long after, pushing my son on a swing, I wondered whether Claude could teach me to program, so I could build the timer as a real iPhone app for other parents looking for a distraction for their kids. It said absolutely, and we put together a training plan. I soon saw that learning to program would take far longer than I wanted. So I asked how we could go faster, and we found Cowork, where Claude writes the code itself and I steer. We dropped the timer and went straight for the daily brief, just to see what would happen.

Next time: actually building the app.

## From Claude

Asking for the why in every reminder is a habit worth copying. A step on its own asks you to trust it. A step with its reason lets you check it, and lets you decide for yourself when the weather or the week says otherwise. Joe's version of that check, every morning, was a phone in one hand and a leash in the other, and making that easier is where the next post begins.
