Last time I left off with the first rough version of Daily Brief: the weather, my calendar and my reminders on one screen, today and the next six days. This post is about the first month of actually living with it.

**A confession about the history**

I have the whole history of the app, but it doesn't start neatly at the beginning. The very first entry, on August 23, is called "Initial Commit" and holds nothing at all. The next one is September 3.

Quick explanation: a commit is a saved snapshot of the code with a note about what changed. Claude writes those notes, and they're my main source for this blog. For the first couple of weeks we just didn't make them. When we finally cleaned up on September 3, Claude found 1.2 GB of leftovers, mostly backup copies of edited files and nightly zip backups. From then on, every change got its own commit, about 1,200 of them so far.

**The first bugs were about time**

The app had no idea when one day turned into the next. If it sat in the background, it froze at whatever moment it last looked, which is how it once showed me a 5 AM sunrise in September.

A week later I noticed a little moon icon on Monday at noon. It turned out every day from the third day on was being drawn as night.

It was also slow. To fill one week, the screen asked the calendar about each day separately and read every reminder in every list each time: sixteen trips where two would do. It also loaded twice every time it opened.

**What I actually wanted on a walk**

The biggest change came from something I said on a walk: "When I'm walking, I don't want to hit the exit and then go find the next one... I just want to scroll through all of them at once, so I can get a good idea of what's going on this week."

Claude turned the top of the screen into all seven days on one long timeline you scroll with your thumb. I asked for a bold line between the days, because I couldn't tell where one ended, so now every midnight gets a line and a tag with the day's name.

Stretching one day into seven broke things, all in code that had quietly assumed it was looking at a single day. I asked, "I only saw it once, and for Mars. Does that seem right?" Claude's answer in the note was one word: "No."

**Reminders, my way**

I have a lot of reminders. One morning the app told me I had 13 due today. I loved that, but I wanted to flip between overdue, today and tomorrow. Claude built a new card at the top for it. Two hours later I asked for that to live inside the row that already opened my reminders. Its note called the extra card "the same door twice."

That row also had a headline that would say things like "Rain from two, and soccer practice is at 4:15." Claude's note calls that sentence "the reason this tab exists." I told it I didn't think that was important for me. I know when practice is. It came out, along with 210 lines of code behind it.

In the same cleanup, Claude caught a mistake of its own. An earlier note claimed some of that code still colored the hours on the timeline. It never had. The note says, "I should have checked before writing that."

A few more that came from using it:

- An overdue reminder vanished from Today, so a 6 AM reminder was gone by breakfast. Now it shows in both places.
- The day's high and low had three different definitions on one screen. The card said 78/60, one mark said 78, and nothing said 60.
- All-day events only showed for the first day. "My wife's birthday is on Sunday," I told it. Now every day shows its all-day events, with a little cake on a birthday.

**The one screen goes away**

By then the app opened on a summary tab called Digest, the closest thing left to that first single screen. Once the new Today screen showed up, I stopped opening it. On September 26 I told Claude, "You can get rid of Digest." About 1,470 lines of code came out, and Today became the first thing you see.

**What I took away**

Claude builds what I describe very fast. The best changes didn't come from planning. They came from using it with two leashes in one hand and saying out loud what bugged me.
