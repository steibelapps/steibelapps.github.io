title: "Thirteen tabs is too many"

By early September, Daily Brief had grown fast. Claude's note from September 10 says it plainly: "The bottom bar had grown past the point where it did its job."

There were thirteen tabs along the bottom of the screen, all fighting for room. This post is about turning that pile into something that feels like a real app.

**Folders, like on a home screen**

The fix was folders. Tabs that belong together now share one button, and a row of small pills at the top picks between them. Calendar, Reminders and Commute became Schedule. Space, Wildlife and Garden became Nature. At first, seven buttons still spilled past the edge of a phone by a whisker, so Claude squeezed them until they fit.

**Two tabs that didn't make it**

The first was Hunt, with hunting seasons. Seasons change every year, state by state. Claude's note says one state alone came to 8,395 combinations checked against one PDF, and getting it wrong wouldn't just be out of date. It could tell somebody they're allowed to shoot something they aren't.

The second was Health. My objection was simple: "I don't want to be the one responsible for giving them false information." Claude tried reworking it to be about the weather, like UV and air quality, instead of about you. On September 8 we retired both. The Commute tab took their place for a while, then got retired too in October, and its warnings moved onto calendar events that have a location, as a travel time.

**One shape for every card**

Tap anything in the app, a planet or a bird or a tomato, and it opens a card. At first every card was one long scroll. Claude's note named the problem: "a scroll hides its own contents." You couldn't tell a card had a section on size until you'd scrolled past four others.

Now every card has a row of pills across the top. A planet's pills are Tonight, Size, Distance, Conditions and About, in that order. One tap takes you straight to the part you want. Claude's example: "2,377 km" says nothing, but "two-thirds the width of our Moon" says everything.

The cards also rise from the bottom edge of the screen now, with no rounded corners at the bottom. Claude's reason: "A plant coming out of soil has a top and no bottom."

**Small things that make it feel finished**

Dark mode was broken. When my phone switched to dark, the tab bar flipped and everything above it stayed cream.

Then there's spelling. Claude had been writing a lot of British English. On October 2 it changed 2,860 words across 59 files to American spelling. A second pass went after the words themselves: fortnight, courgette, torch, and dates written like "20 July 1969."

I wanted to move cards around myself. "I think the cards in all of the tabs should be rearrangeable," I said. "For weather, I'd like to be able to drag the conditions to the top." Now a long press on any card lets you arrange them.

And every card had to earn its place. The Discover card showed me rosemary with a blurb about it being a perennial in zone eight. I told Claude "we need a reason to have it on the Discover today card," and to go through all of the cards. That turned into 211 new one-line facts for the plants, plus a "why this is worth knowing" line on cards across the app.

**The welcome screen, both ways**

In October Claude built a welcome screen: a little window on the real sky, with the Sun or Moon where it actually is, clouds drifting with the wind, rain falling from them, and a joke. There are 199 holiday lines for 21 days of the year.

It was pretty. It was also slow. By the end of October it was staying up 12 to 17 seconds while the app loaded everything behind it. I told Claude, "Make everything open really quickly. The welcome screen should be cosmetic and not needed." Claude cut the launch work way down and the welcome screen to three seconds. Then I looked again and told Claude, "It's still pretty to look at," so now it's six seconds, and a tap skips it.

**Less cluttered**

On October 30 I asked for the tabs to be "less cluttered." The header went from three rows to two, Settings became a gear next to the search bar, and the bar at the bottom is down to five tabs.

On October 9 the app got a version number, 1.0, so it could go to the App Store. As for the icon, my official decision was "the app icon is fine for now."
